PART 1

Campaign foundation

1 · Campaign name

THE SECOND IT GOES IN.
Already living in the brand's own copy ("built for the second it goes in"), so the campaign inherits language the site speaks natively. Short form for on-screen text: THE SECOND.

2 · Central idea

Every goal compresses a lifetime of feeling into roughly one second: the inhale before, the impact, the eruption after. Nobody remembers what they were wearing. GOOOL exists for the people who live for that second anyway. The campaign slows that second down, frame by frame, for 30 days, and dresses it.

3 · Manifesto (pinned post copy, also voiceover for the anthem reel)

There is one second every fan on earth understands. The stadium inhales. The living room stands up. The street goes quiet. Then it goes in. Nobody practices what they do in that second. It just comes out of you. We make clothing for the people who live for it. Four pieces. One mark. No noise. GOOOL. Made for the Moment.

4 · Customer insight

Football fans in the US live between two wardrobes: replica kits that feel like costumes off the pitch, and streetwear that says nothing about the game they love. The insight: people want to carry the feeling of football without wearing a team. A goal is the one experience that belongs to every fan of every club and no federation. GOOOL owns the emotion, not the allegiance.

5 · Emotional promise

Wear the second you live for. When someone who knows the game sees the crest, they feel the roar without a word being said.

6 · Functional promise (spec-verified only)

  • Performance Badge Tee: 3.8 oz recycled polyester with PosiCharge, athletic fit, taped neck. Built to train in.
  • Core Hoodie: true 10 oz heavyweight fleece, cotton face, generous fit. Structure you can feel.
  • Casual Wordmark Tee: 6.5 oz garment-dyed cotton, relaxed fit, lived-in finish out of the box.
  • Touchline Cap: firm buckram front, structured five-panel, curved visor, flat embroidery.
  • All decoration centered, all backs blank, one mark. That restraint is the design story.

8 · Message hierarchy

  1. The feeling: the second it goes in belongs to everyone. (campaign layer)
  2. The mark: one crest, one wordmark, no team, no noise. (brand layer)
  3. The garments: real weights, real fabrics, centered decoration, blank backs. (product layer)
  4. The action: the First Capsule is coming; the list hears first. (conversion layer)

Every post leads with layer 1 or 3 and closes with layer 4. Layer 2 is carried visually, not written.

9 · Voice and caption guidelines

  • Short lines. Full sentences. No em dashes. Middle dot as separator: GOOOL · Made for the Moment.
  • Confident, never hype. Say "heavyweight," never "insane quality." Never beg ("smash that follow").
  • One CTA per caption, always the same act during prelaunch: Join the list at goool.shop. Vary the sentence, never the destination.
  • Captions 1–4 lines for reels, up to 8 for carousels and brand-story posts. First line must survive truncation.
  • Hashtags: 3–6, chosen per post from the bank in Part 5. Never the same block twice running.
  • Spell the brand GOOOL in text, always.

10–11 · Visual direction

Palette. Black garments, white mark, one red accent per frame maximum (the crest bar, the underline, a red thread on set). Environments in concrete grey, pitch green at dusk, natural cream (the cap's second color). Never rainbow, never busy.

Lighting. Hard single-source light with real shadows (phone flash, one LED panel, or low sun). Night pitch floodlights. Indoor: one window, everything else off. The brand look is contrast, not glow.

Framing. Verticals 4:5 and 9:16, centered subjects, generous negative space above. Product macro shots dead-center, mirroring the centered-decoration design rule. Grid coherence: alternate dark frame / light frame so the profile checkerboards.

Texture. Fleece loops, twill weave, embroidery thread, DTF ink sheen, chain-link, worn goal netting, concrete. Shoot texture close enough to feel it.

Environment. Empty pitches at golden hour and under lights, stadium tunnels and stands (public/park level, no pro venues), locker benches, city walks, a couch on matchday. Never a trophy-room fantasy.

Casting. Real players and real fans, 20s–30s, athletic but not fitness-model staged. Faces optional; hands, backs of heads, and silhouettes carry half the campaign. Style with what the audience owns: black shorts, faded denim, white socks, real boots.

13 · Organic-to-paid journey

Organic reels seed the emotion → best performers (saves + 75% watch) get promoted as awareness ads within 72 hours → profile visitors and 50%+ video viewers enter retargeting → retargeting serves product-truth carousels and the waitlist ad → goool.shop email capture → email owns the launch moment. Paid never runs creative that organic hasn't validated, except the two purpose-built waitlist ads in Part 3.

14 · Three pinned posts

  1. Pin 1 · The anthem reel (Day 1). The manifesto over sound and dark frames. Introduces the feeling.
  2. Pin 2 · The mark carousel (Day 4). Crest and wordmark anatomy: what it means, why there is no team. Introduces the brand.
  3. Pin 3 · The First Capsule carousel (Day 9). All four garments, real specs, real prices, waitlist CTA. Introduces the clothing.

15 · How "The Sound of Victory" is used

It is the campaign's audio identity, not a slogan on repeat. Rules: it appears in exactly five places across 30 days: the Day 1 anthem, the Day 8 sound-design reel built entirely around it, the Day 15 mark film, one Story series (Day 18), and the Day 29 close. In audio it is the crowd roar itself; in text it appears title-case, once per piece, never as a hashtag, never as a product claim (nothing in the capsule carries it as decoration). Between those five moments the campaign expresses the same idea through craft: sound design of stadium air, inhales, silence before impact. The phrase stays rare so it stays loaded.
